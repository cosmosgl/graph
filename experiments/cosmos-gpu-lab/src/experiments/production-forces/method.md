Both canvases load their workspace Graph package into an isolated frame. Each receives the same public setters, random seed, fixed camera and paused graph. Step once advances one update. Run to the final tick advances the same fixture in a batch. Reset recreates both Graph instances.

The seven small single-force fixtures each use eight point slots. The orange point is pinned at its input coordinate. An absent point has two NaN coordinates, and the six remaining points can move. The tests check every coordinate at ticks 0, 1, 4 and 8. They independently check each force's direction or expected first-step position, the pinned coordinates, the absent slot and finite values everywhere else. The coordinate budget is 0.01 graph units per axis, a hundredth of a CSS pixel at this camera scale. The results also report exact agreement.

Gravity takes its first tick through an unpaused, host-driven frame to check that this public entry point advances an active simulation.

The pointer fixture sends right-button movement at a known canvas position. It alternates a manual step with a paused render frame for four updates, releases the button, then checks the positions again at tick 8. The weighted springs include an absent endpoint and a zero-strength edge. Attractor and cluster cases include varying weights and disabled inputs. The cluster case uses both an explicit center and a center computed from live points.

The hierarchical fixture has 8,192 slots in an asymmetrical cloud. Both graphs use their own spatial approximation above the 4,096-slot direct-repulsion threshold. The comparison reads their layouts at ticks 0, 1, 4, 8, 16, 31 and 32. RMS position error is the root-mean-square distance between corresponding live points. RMS radius measures the spread of each layout around its own centroid. At each checkpoint the error and radius difference must each stay below 5% of the WebGL radius. By tick 32, both radii must exceed 1.5 times their initial values and each final single-step RMS motion must drop below 5% of its first step.

## Springs, repulsion and collision

The combined case has seven present points and one absent slot. Point 0 is pinned. The two free points 1 and 2 start 8.944 graph units apart. Their diameters are 28 and 18. With 2 units of padding on each radius, contact occurs at 27 units. The five links have different spring strengths and a fixed rest length of 44. The fixture uses direct repulsion and two collision iterations per tick. The camera, seed, strengths and typed arrays are the same in both packages.

At ticks 0, 1, 4 and 8 the comparison checks the pin, absence, finite coordinates and the same 0.01-unit per-axis budget as the isolated small fixtures. It measures the RMS distance between corresponding points, movement since the preceding checkpoint, the remaining penetration of points 1 and 2, and the lengths of links 0 and 1. Penetration is the positive part of 27 minus the measured separation. A repeated WebGL trajectory establishes the control. Runs with each force disabled separately check that all three contribute.

The original WebGL midpoint of link 0 rounds to (72, 84) CSS pixels. At tick 8 it rounds to (79, 61). Each engine draws from its own simulated positions. Both receive the same two clicks and a third at (44, 40), where the orange point covers its incident links. The checks read the rendered colors at those coordinates and the IDs reported by the public click callbacks before and after movement. Resetting and running eight ticks in one batch or eight individual steps must reproduce each engine's positions, colors and picks.
